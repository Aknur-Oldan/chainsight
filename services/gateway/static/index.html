Bank ⇄ Urząd · MITM-proof Gateway

Customer PII never leaves the bank.
No man-in-the-middle can touch it.

The bank must report customer data to a government office (urząd). PII is tokenized at the bank's edge, every packet is HMAC-signed with a timestamp and nonce — tampering or replaying it in transit is rejected on arrival. A breach at the urząd yields only random tokens, mass detokenization auto-revokes the key, and every event lands on a tamper-evident hash chain.

1 · Report customer data to the urząd

The bank sends records through the gateway. PII fields (name, pesel, iban, phone, email) are swapped for random tokens, then the packet is HMAC-signed before leaving the perimeter.

2 · Man-in-the-middle attacks

An attacker between the bank and the urząd tampers with the signed packet (swaps an IBAN for his own) or replays a captured packet. The urząd verifies signature + nonce and rejects both.

3 · Breach the urząd

Simulate a breach of the government office: dump everything it stores. This is all an attacker would exfiltrate.

4 · Legitimate detokenization

The urząd exchanges a token for the real value via the bank's policy-enforced API. Paste a token from step 1.

5 · Mass exfiltration attempt

An attacker with the urząd's stolen API key tries to bulk-detokenize. The rate-limit policy fires and the key is automatically revoked.
